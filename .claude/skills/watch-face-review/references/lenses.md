# The four lenses

Append one of these to the shared brief for each reviewer.

## Typography and elegance

Your lens: TYPOGRAPHY AND ELEGANCE. Judge font choice and pairing, sizes
and the hierarchy between them (time > date > readings > captions),
weights, letter case, text colours, how each label reads, whether units sit
right against their numbers, the icons' drawing quality and whether they
match each other in size and weight, and whether the whole thing looks
refined rather than busy. Say exactly which font, size (as a fraction of
screen width) and colour each text element should use.

## Spacing, balance and feng shui

Your lens: SPACING, BALANCE AND FENG SHUI. Measure, don't eyeball: use
Pillow on the 1x PNGs to find each element's bounding box (lit text is much
brighter than background decoration) and report the vertical rhythm (gaps
between every band), left/right symmetry, the ink's centre against the
face's axis, distances from each element to the round edge, visual weight
top versus bottom, and clearance from every element to the nearest
decoration. Count sharp bends and loose line ends if the face has drawn
lines. Give target positions as fractions of the screen. Scratch scripts go
in your own scratchpad folder.

## Nature and art direction (or the face's theme)

Your lens: ART DIRECTION. The owner wants <THEME, e.g. "a very
nature-esque feel ... really artsy and beautiful">. Judge the palette
(contrast ratios of decoration against black, whether accents shout),
whether each reading feels part of the picture or like a UI widget pasted
on, the day and night moods, and every colour option in the settings sheet
(distinct from each other, on-theme, readings still clear). Propose
concrete, implementable changes (hex colours, forms) without hurting
legibility.

## The skeptic: glanceability, mess, completeness

Your lens: THE SKEPTIC. Be the owner glancing at the wrist for one second.
Can each required value be read instantly and unambiguously (digits that
could be misread, icons that could mean two things, two options that look
alike, a reading with no cue or unit)? What still looks messy or noisy?
Check always-on (pixels lit, legibility), the 416 px render (clipping,
sizes) and, once there is one, that the simulator capture matches the
previews. Compare with the BEFORE state: is it genuinely better, and is
anything lost?
